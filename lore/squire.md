---
name: The Squire
epithet: Fighter (Banneret)
---

A happy-go-lucky, ambitious presence in the village, the Squire is a bright spot in a dark corner of the world. She has declared herself a squire, though she has yet to find a knight to serve. She was dead certain the Flagellant would take her under her wing. That hasn't happened... yet!

Determined and good-humored, the Squire is quick to make friends. She'd be quicker still if she took off her helmet, which badly muffles her voice and often nearly slips off her head. Her bag holds everything her yet-undetermined mentor could want: plenty of food (which she won't eat, for fear of being unprepared for a starving, wayward knight), several pieces of half-plate for repairs (which she refuses to wear until she's a knight herself), spare shields and weapons (kept pristine for her knight), and a scatter of ledgers, diaries, and drawings.

She followed the Flagellant to the village, admiring how the older woman could win over all kinds of folk despite her shocking headwear. Like the Knight-errant once did, the Squire mostly runs mundane chores for the townsfolk, but she takes real pleasure in them. Some villagers even salute her as she passes: "My liege!"

She has met the mounting monster attacks with vigor, finding a more knightly purpose in repelling them. With a shortsword and a heater shield, she fights more like a proper man-at-arms than a meek squire.

## Secret

The Squire is a wanted criminal where she comes from. She built this identity to escape detection, and her helmet does most of the work, hiding her face behind its visor. Like the Flagellant, she killed a man of high standing: a noble lord, in whose palace she worked as a lowly cook.

She once worked up the courage to ask him to take her as his squire. He laughed. He laughed in front of the whole household ("A cook? A *squire*?") and sent her back to the kitchens. That night she poisoned his supper, choosing a poison that would not kill him, only still him. She dragged him before his own hearth, took his helmet from its stand, and put it on. Then she knelt beside him, close enough for him to see her through the visor, and for once he did not laugh. When she was finished, she bludgeoned him into a grisly display of his inner spirit.

The helmet she never takes off is his. She has since stripped its crest, hammered out his arms, and crowned it with a cheap red plume, and to a stranger it is just a plain old helm. Anyone from his household who looked closely would know it. She keeps it anyway.

She is earnest in her kindness and in her wish to be a knight. But she has fled so many times that she cannot bear to do it again. Wickermoor is the closest thing to a home she has found.
