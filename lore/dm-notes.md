## The truth

**The Green Mother was once a fair god.** In an earlier age she was benevolent, with servants among the fae, and the villagers of Wickermoor still worship her as a distant, Mother Nature-like deity.

**She was defeated by Philip Druskenvald,** the plane's new ruler and its namesake. Weakened, and filled with mounting hatred for what the plane has become, she allowed an eldritch force to elevate her magic, at the cost of partly corrupting her mind.

**She is now half fae, half eldritch:** voice-tormented, paranoid and power-hungry. She has let the eyes of the eldritch suffuse the soil and air of Wickermoor Hollow, the last domain she holds. She means to retake Druskenvald, starting with an attack on Wickermoor Village. She is the force pressing against the edges of the village, and the source of the strange magic in the soil.

**Not all her servants followed her.** At least one fae has fallen and turned against her (see the Exiled).

**The monsters are Wickermoor's missing villagers,** changed by what the Green Mother has let into the soil and air. The Pomander has proof; the Wall has a single ring.

**The moon has risen like this before.** The Wretched is proof: he was a village cat who looked at the moon the last time.

## Places

- **Wickermoor Hollow**: the province.
- **Wickermoor Village**: the town where the one-shot takes place. Recently expanded toward the Wytchwood. The village map (`story elements/wickermore_village.html`) describes the campaign's present day, 10-15 years after the one-shot: age its people back, and leave out those who arrive later (Finneas Trout, Weston Murdoch and the Brightsingers, Jean Myriel). The church is still a bare ruin; the Crooked House is still the abandoned Lockwood Estate.
- **The barracks**: the Watch's barracks on the village's west edge, by the smithy (V16), facing the Wytchwood. Where the one-shot begins.
- **The bell tower**: the Haunted Clocktower (V25), attached to the mayor's home and office, facing the Founder's Round.
- **The Wytchwood**: the forest beside the village. Few return from it.
- **Olamarsh**: land of the Deepborn. Its cultists overran the Relentless's home hamlet and carried most of his extended family off for sacrifice. He escaped with only his wife and youngest son.

## People

- **The governor**: Mayor Wendel Somerton, 10-15 years younger than on the village map. Secretly replaced by a doppelganger during the one-shot, which carries into the campaign (who makes it, and how, is still to be decided). A weak-minded man pushing Wickermoor Village outward toward the Wytchwood, against the forest's clear resistance. Uses the Relentless as a puppet to play warrior through. Secretly sick with the early stages of the villagers' change; kept lucid by the Pomander's tincture. His expansion is what put the village near the beast's nest (see the Knight-errant).
- **Siv Harga, the sniper**: the butcher (V15), a scout and one of the Fold of the Patient Lady. On the Wickery's rooftop lookout (V17) with a longbow, guarding the chandler's family below, including a young Oswick Frey, who can't run. She has seen neighbours walk up the street sounding like themselves, so she shoots anything that moves, the party included. See "The sniper" below.
- **The voice in the bell tower**: the Green Mother, speaking through a stolen voice. She also controls the bells, which the village trusts to tell it when it's safe.

## Character threads

- **Sorceress**: born of a bargain her parents made with the Green Mother's emissary. **The price was her:** she would be raised and her gift grown, and when the moon returned, the Green Mother would collect the harvest. **That's tonight.** At the climax she chooses: let the fae take back all her magic and walk away ordinary, or become the vessel, an elegant, intelligent eldritch herald of the Green Mother (think mind flayer, not mindless abomination; fits her Aberrant Mind powers). She knows only that she suspects the emissary, that the hunger is growing, and that her birthmark matches the Summoner's flask. Omens she's seen but can't explain: a Wytchwood beast bowed to her in a moonlit clearing (they recognize their herald); her birthmark's half-moon is filling in as the moon swells (a countdown: full tonight); her mother wrote begging her to come home before the harvest moon (her parents know the price).
  - **Feed the Hunger** (on her sheet): bonus action, take 2d6 + 2 psychic damage (can't be reduced), regain 2 sorcery points. **Each use = 1 Corruption, tracked secretly by the DM**: tell the player "I'm marking a point of corruption." More Corruption makes the herald path harder to refuse.
- **Guttersnipe & Stargazer**: the only ones who know an eldritch attack is coming. The Guttersnipe secretly wants to see it.
- **Knight-errant**: secretly crippled a mutated beast's young instead of killing the beast; its black blood gave her fae magic and wings that pull her toward the Wytchwood. Publicly, the village believes she slew it and that the Green Mother rewarded her with wings; the governor parades her at his speeches. Her radiant light is moonlight, strongest when the moon is. The grieving beast is still out there.
- **Flagellant**: secretly murdered her cruel knight mentor in his sleep; the Wytchwood's soil (the Green Mother's) turned his body into the living thorn crown on her head. What's left of him hates her mercy: the thorns tighten and draw blood every time she spares a foe. They've grown since the Green Mother's power swelled; the voice she hears at night is his, carrying whatever is whispering to the maddened villagers. (Publicly, players know only that she hears something too.) Publicly, she's in Wickermoor undoing the damage the knight did here, and has noticed villagers wailing, beating their heads on the gates, and biting.
- **Wretched**: moon-turned cat. A voice in the village promises him a home.
- **Squire**: killed the noble lord she cooked for after he publicly laughed at her wish to be his squire. Her helmet is the dead lord's, disguised with a red plume; anyone from his household who looked closely would recognize it. Her would-be mentor, the Flagellant, also secretly killed the man she squired for (the Squire's secret hints at this), but doesn't know the Squire's story. Every time the Flagellant declines to take her on, it echoes the laugh.
- **Bird Keeper**: really does live in the Wytchwood, with her son, whom the forest turned into a hulking beast. She tamed him and still reads to him. She's in the village to turn the guard and governor from expansion. Her son keeps following her to the barracks. Her sparrows are her spy network: some stay with her son, some carry warnings to the Relentless, some listen at the governor's hall. The Wretched's only real friend.
- **Relentless**: founder of the guard. Secretly helps the Bird Keeper lead her son home whenever he comes to the barracks, because he couldn't save his other children. His wife and youngest son are in the barracks tonight, under guard. His absences are why the guard is falling apart and the attacks are getting worse. Friends with the Exiled.
- **Exiled**: dying of a wound from a cursed greatsword, now the blade on his back. Its previous host was the bandit warlord he killed; the sword chose the Exiled as a younger, stronger vessel. It holds a fallen fae, once a servant of the Green Mother, sworn to find a host strong enough to level the land she clings to. The poison eats his mind, and raging speeds it. He came to Wickermoor to bargain with the Green Mother for a cure; the fae wanted to come here too.
- **Stalwart**: an acorn from a family of treant-like tree-folk. His kin grew mighty from the Hollow's soil; he never drank of it, so the Green Mother has no roots in him, and may be unable to touch him. His grandfather's old friend, a giant tree, lives in the village.
- **Summoner**: the demon in her flask is her brother's soul bound into a long-dead demon's body; the two share one mind. Her rituals keep him uppermost; the old bloodbaths were the demon winning. She's right that the Wytchwood's monsters were once gentler things.
- **Stargazer**: Gnarlborn, one of the tree-folk said to be born of the Green Mother. Knows her full story and the coming attack (as does the Guttersnipe). His divine light comes from her, and he fears he's already hers. Hasn't bloomed this year. His partner is alive.
- **Swordsaint**: carries the spirits of her lineage in her cloak, one per sword. Hunting ancient swords lodged in the Wytchwood's eldest monsters, which puts her at odds with the Summoner, Bird Keeper, Relentless and Knight-errant. Some of her mentors' voices seem compromised. On their orders she crippled a guard, and the monsters struck his empty post that night; the Relentless wrongly blames himself. There is one more voice in her cloak than there should be.
- **Wall**: silkborn who believes he's the guard's secret leader and tells everyone. Really a military reject back home; the governor's men send him on every mission because he's expendable. Ambushed at the post left empty by the guard the Swordsaint crippled. A grieving beast gave him a wedding ring belonging to a missing villager, so he knows the monsters are villagers, and has told no one.
- **Pomander**: a real noble, disgraced after a disease he was studying escaped his lab and ravaged his family's lands; the mask is guilt as much as protection. Secretly keeps the sick governor lucid with a tincture, partly to save the village, partly for a promised letter restoring his name. His bestiary proves the monsters are mutated villagers (birthmarks, dental quirks, personal items), that they coordinate, and that their gatherings follow the moon. He has calculated that the greatest gathering is tonight. The change travels through soil and air; his mask has kept him clean.

## Running the one-shot

About 6 hours over two sessions. Inspirations: *Bird Box* (crossing blind; the lures use loved ones' voices), *Local 58* (official instructions that slowly turn), *The Mist*, *30 Days of Night*, *The Thing* (who's still human?).

**Vibe:** a slow-burn siege that sometimes breaks into panic. Indoors should feel *almost* safe; outdoors should feel like leaving the boat. **The chaos rises all night:** every haven is worse than the last, and the prowl clock gets shorter.

### The route

The loop is **haven → crossing → haven**, west to east across the village, ending with the tower.

| # | Beat | Where | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Session 1** | | | |
| 1 | The barracks: opening, the criers, the first bell, the first breach | Barracks (near V16) | 45m |
| 2 | Crossing 1: learning the moon, the prowlers, moving blind | Lanes east | 30m |
| 3 | Haven 1: pleading doors, the patient ones, the first lure | Harga's Meats / Apothecary (V15, V6) | 60m |
| 4 | Crossing 2: under Siv's fire, ending on a cliffhanger | Toward the Wickery (V17) | 30m |
| **Session 2** | | | |
| 5 | The sniper (midpoint): talk Siv down; she becomes an ally | Wickery rooftop (V17) | 45m |
| 6 | Haven 2: everything at once | Green Queen Inn (V1) or Schoolhouse (V8) | 60m |
| 7 | The last crossing: the Founder's Round, the Oak of Many Faces, Siv covering them | V9, V10 | 30m |
| 8 | The tower: the voice, the climax, the Sorceress's choice and the other payoffs | Clocktower (V25) | 60m |

To cut time, merge Haven 1 and Haven 2.

### The opening (the barracks)

Use rushed ambience and slow, slightly eerie music. In order:
1. **Wonder.** The moon hangs so low it seems to fill the sky. Confused villagers wander the streets. Looking feels safe this early. Ask the players whether their characters look, and quietly note who does (see "The infection").
2. **The wounded arrive.** Villagers are carried to the barracks infirmary with bites and terrible headaches, raving about monsters at the gate. Some say a relative turned on them. The Relentless's wife and son are sheltering here under guard.
3. **The crier.** *"Don't go outside. Don't look at the moon."* The party gets the first notice (handout 1) and helps board doors and windows. The Relentless pulls the party together. If no one picked the Relentless, he's an NPC with his hands full, and another unpicked character says a search party needs to go out.
4. **The bell.** It rings unbidden, and the voice calls down from the tower for help. Now the party has a goal.
5. **The infirmary turns.** Everyone brought in was already infected. The first breach comes **from inside**, and staying is no longer an option.

### Crossings (outdoors)

Three pressures stack up:
- **The moon:** each round outdoors with eyes uncovered, make a WIS save or look. Covering your eyes is safe but leaves you effectively blinded.
- **Siv:** one arrow per round at whatever moves fastest or makes the most noise. Players hear the bowstring first, so they learn to fear it. Moving slowly means more rounds under the moon.
- **Prowlers:** they close in on noise. Fighting in the street draws more of them, and Siv's arrows.

**Voices in the dark.** Things speak to the party as they cross. A blindfolded party hears gasping footsteps coming closer and can't tell whether it's a villager pleading or a prowler. Make it personal where you can. For the **Squire**, someone shouts for her, says they need to see her helmet, then walks up and tears at it (a grapple). If the helmet comes off or someone gets a good look at it, a voice says *"That's his."* Every PC with a secret can get a version of this.

**The lure** is the Green Mother's eye: a lantern in a window, a glow at the end of an alley, a loved one's voice. It shows each PC what their secret wants, and always leads somewhere exposed (open ground, Siv's line of fire, the Oak). Examples:
- **Bird Keeper:** her son, hurt, calling for her from the square.
- **Relentless:** his lost children's voices from a cellar. Later in the night, his youngest son's voice, though the boy should be safe in the barracks.
- **Squire:** the dead lord's household crest on a figure who beckons, as if they recognise the helmet.
- **Flagellant:** a wounded foe begging mercy.
- **Sorceress:** beasts kneeling in a ring of moonlight.
- **Exiled:** the sword's fae pulling toward a "cure."

### Havens (indoors) and the prowl clock

The prowl clock is **visible to the players**. One clock per haven, and it shrinks as the night goes on:

| Haven | Hour | Clock size | Time tick |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barracks | Evening | 8 | every 15 min real time |
| Haven 1 | Late Evening | 6 | every 10 min |
| The Wickery | Midnight | 6 | every 10 min; **Siv's arrows hold one segment back each round** once she's on side |
| Haven 2 | Witching Hour | 4 | every 5 min |
| The tower | | none, they're already inside | |

**Ticks** (1 segment unless noted): noise, a light facing the street, opening a door or window, a scream, a failed group stealth check. Starting a fight indoors costs 2.

**A full clock means a breach, not instant death.** The prowlers come through the weakest point. The party gets **one round** to reach an exit they planned, and the haven is lost. Anyone left behind becomes the next door that pleads.

**Temptations to stay too long.** Each is worth having, and each has a cost on the clock:

| Temptation | Cost | Twist |
|---|---|---|
| Short rest | 3 segments (half the clock in Haven 2) | Interrupted means no benefit |
| A villager begging to be led out | Free to agree; slower and noisier on the next crossing | Some are already bitten |
| The Summoner's ritual | 2 segments | Skipping it lets the demon rise a little |
| A mutated villager threatening to cry out | Silencing them takes a check or a kill | Killing a villager is murder; the Flagellant's thorns tighten if she spares them; the Wall may recognise them |
| Crying behind a locked door | 1 segment to pick or force it | Real about half the time |
| The lure light in a window | 0 segments, but costs the PC's position | Leads somewhere exposed |
| Supplies (apothecary reagents, tincture ingredients) | 1 segment per search | The only way to slow the infection |

**What fills a haven:** pleading doors (some voices are true; some *were* true an hour ago), breaches (a shutter swinging open, a wall giving way, a cellar door never barred), and the patient ones (villagers waiting in corners or under beds, not attacking until someone comes close). Haven 1 has isolated threats with clear choices. Haven 2 has everything at once: a breach already under way, two villagers begging (one turning), the rewritten notice on the wall, and the four knocks.

### The infection (DM only)

A bite starts a hidden three-stage track. **Never show it to the players.** Every PC who looked at the moon during the opening also starts with one hidden point toward stage 1. They'll only work out why when the criers start shouting.
1. Fever and hunger. Pass the player a note: *"You're very hungry."*
2. The lures speak to that PC in a loved one's voice, with disadvantage to resist. Their copy of the procedures now reads differently (hand them an infection slip from the handouts).
3. The PC turns at the climax.

Siv's test, the Pomander's tincture and the Stalwart's immunity (the Green Mother has no roots in him) are each ways to find out who is infected.

### The sniper (midpoint)

Siv is resolved by **talking**. Her rule: the party has to **prove they're still human**, with one question, one test or one thing to show her. Infected PCs make this tense, because she may be right about one of them. Ways in: a sparrow carrying a message (Bird Keeper), animal handling or calm (Siv knows beasts), the Relentless's name (she'd know the guard's founder), or simply standing still in the open.

Once she's an ally, she holds back the prowl clock at the Wickery, gives overwatch at the Founder's Round (calls out prowlers, shoots at the Oak), and does her best in the final conflict, though she can't follow them into the tower.

### The climax (top of the clocktower)

**The boss** is inspired by Lady Maria of the Astral Clocktower (*Bloodborne*). She's an **emissary of the Green Mother**: a bargained child like the Sorceress, who chose the herald path and sided with the Green Mother. She shows the Sorceress what she'd become, right before the Sorceress has to choose. She lashes with a whip and, in some rounds, uses frantic, blooming fae magic.

**Second phase:** the clock face shatters, and **the moon is behind it**. From then on, everyone fights in full moonlight, either blind or looking. Pale, red-tipped hands reach through the broken face, grabbing frantically at the characters, and a couple of eldritch creatures pour in with them.

**Endings depend on who's left standing** (one climax beat per picked character, written once the roster is final). So far:
- **Exiled:** if he's defeated or turned, the sword compels him to run himself through. He becomes a huntable NPC with the blade still in him, a seed for the campaign. Whoever pulls it out becomes its next host.
- **Guttersnipe:** can choose to glimpse the unthinkable. A wave of force rolls out over the village, and through the broken clock face it reaches the arena too. Either it rapidly mutates everyone outdoors, the PCs included, or it pulls the Guttersnipe apart at the seams, turns him into an arcane elemental, and sends him to the Astral Plane. That leads to a short solo sequence inspired by *Outer Wilds*. Keep it to 5-10 minutes and run it as a post-credits scene, after everyone else's endings are settled.
- **Sorceress:** see "Character threads".

### The procedures handout

Printables are in `story elements/handouts.html`. The party's own copy **never changes**. Only the copies they find do, and comparing the two is how they catch the lies. Rule 9 ("Pray to the Green Mother") is sincere, which is what makes it terrible. Rule 8's "three bells, be ready to open" is the code the voice exploits.

| When | Where | What has changed |
|---|---|---|
| Evening, Haven 1 | Notice on the butcher's door | An added **10.** in the same hand: *"The Watch will knock four times. Let them in."* Later, something knocks four times. |
| Crossing 2 | A crier walking calmly down the middle of the street | *"Amendment. Rule 5 is rescinded. Crossing blind is how they take you. Keep your eyes open."* |
| Witching Hour, Haven 2 | Notice inside the inn | Rule 1 adds *"…until the bells tell you."* Rule 9 becomes *"**Go** to the Green Mother. She waits for her children in the Founder's Round."* Ink still wet; Somerton's signature. |
| Final crossing | The bells | **Five bells**, a signal not on the list. Then the voice: *"It's safe to look now."* |

### Flashback hooks

Present-day details on the village map that could happen tonight:
- **Morgan the apothecary** "awoke one night in Wickermoor Village as a youngster" with no memory: the party can find that child tonight.
- **Thomasin Ardor** fell through a pane of glass as a child, and Randall Graham stitched her up: a breached window in a house full of children.
- **Holly Aster's mother** dies on her 35th birthday (the family curse): still alive now, and it could be tonight.
- **Somerton's cane**, which he "does not need, but always carries": the real Somerton breaks his leg tonight, and the doppelganger copies the cane.
- **Old Rusty**, **Mrs. Oakum** (werewolf, howling under this moon), and the **Dovecote's pigeons** against the Bird Keeper's sparrows.

## Open questions

- Is the clocktower's emissary the same emissary who brokered the Sorceress's and the Summoner's bargains? What was her name before?
- Whose voice has the Green Mother stolen for the bell tower? Is it the same voice promising the Wretched a home?
- Is the Knight-errant's beast one of the Green Mother's creatures?
- What happens to those who look at the moon tonight?
- What changed the Green Mother from benevolent to what she is now, and what made her fae servant fall?
- Is the giant tree, the Stalwart's grandfather's friend, one of the Green Mother's?
- The Summoner's pact was brokered by the **same emissary** as the Sorceress's: the circle beneath a horned half-moon on her flask is the emissary's mark, and the Sorceress bears it as a birthmark. Does the Summoner know? Is her brother also part of tonight's harvest?
- *Optional:* one of the Guttersnipe's forgotten tomes describes "a child bargained into being, to be harvested when the moon returns." If the Guttersnipe has read it, they know what the Sorceress may become tonight, and part of them wants to watch.
- Where is the Stargazer's partner, and does she know what the Green Mother has become?
- Is the moon one of the "eyes of the eldritch"? It would explain the warning not to look at it.
- The Stalwart is tree-folk too: are Gnarlborn and his family kin?
- **Pinned:** Is the Knight-errant's beast the Bird Keeper's son, and the young it carried her grandchild? If so, the village's hero secretly crippled the Bird Keeper's grandchild.
- Whose is the extra voice in the Swordsaint's cloak: the Green Mother, the eldritch, or the monsters' leader?
- Do the Wytchwood's monsters have a leader coordinating their attacks?
- If the Exiled bargains with the Green Mother, what does the fae in his sword do?
