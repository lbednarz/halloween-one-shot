name: The Pomander
epithet: Fighter (Eldritch Knight)

A dragon-like warrior from afar, the Pomander claims to be a nobleman disgraced from his house. To some in the guard, he is an inside joke: "the play-pretend prince." To those who humor him, he is a talented alchemist, a savvy beast hunter, a scholar, and an unreliable fount of "once upon a time" stories from his spectacular life. He is quick to turn his nose up at commoners who fail to, say, compliment his fine garb or note his air of nobility, and just as quick to be enchanted by anyone who recognizes a noble gentleman when they meet one.

He boasts an arsenal of trinkets: effigies, a spellbook, a beaked mask and hazard clothing, herbs and medicine, and a ferocious warhammer. He is compiling a bestiary of the region's monsters, studying their weaknesses and tracking their habitats and migrations, so he always has the right poison ready at the right moment to turn a fight. Sometimes he even does it all alone.

He says he came to Wickermoor to cultivate its flora and to catalogue its strange creatures.

With a powerful physique and a keen mind, the Pomander makes short work of his foes, detached and brutal, with destructive magic and his fearsome hammer. Blood, guts, and fighting are unbecoming of his station.

The governor is not popular with the guard, yet the Pomander has been seen slipping off to the governor's manor with a full satchel and returning with an empty one. Is he climbing the social ladder? Or is he slipping something into the governor's evening tea?

## Secret

He really was a noble, and he really was disgraced. As a young lord, he dabbled in the study of disease, and something he was keeping in his laboratory got out. It swept through his family's lands before anyone understood what it was. His house buried the scandal by burying his name.

The governor is sick. He shows the earliest signs of whatever is happening to the villagers: the lapses, the fears, the hunger for something he can't name. The satchels hold a tincture that keeps him lucid, for now. The Pomander tells himself it's to keep the village from collapsing. It is also because the governor has promised, once this is over, to write to the Pomander's house and ask that his name be restored.

And his bestiary has shown him what no one else has seen. The monsters of the Wytchwood are Wickermoor's own villagers. He has proven it on the dissecting table: a birthmark he once noticed on a baker's wrist, the crooked tooth of a farmhand who went missing in spring, a locket still tangled in matted fur. The monsters move together now, in coordinated packs, and their gatherings follow the phases of the moon. By his calculations, the greatest gathering yet comes tonight. Whatever is changing the villagers travels through soil and air. Only his mask and tinctures have kept it out of him.
