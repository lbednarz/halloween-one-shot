name: The Flagellant
epithet: Fighter (Battle Master)
summary: A former squire quietly undoing her cruel master's wrongs, wearing his armor and a living crown of thorns. She'd rather talk a foe down than kill them.
---

Once squire to a great knight, the Flagellant watched as her mentor used his power to extort and belittle the weak, feeding his greed and lust at their expense. Under mysterious circumstances, the knight is gone now. She wears his armor: a half-plate set, ill-fitting and massive when she first put it on, which she has since draped in cloak-like coverings, both to hide its worth from ambitious highwaymen and to seem less fearsome to the people she serves. Wound tightly around her head is a sort of living plant that occasionally writhes and cuts into the knight. She does not take it off. She does not talk about it.

She has spent many months in Wickermoor, quietly undoing what she can of her mentor's wicked deeds: returning what he took, mending what he broke, apologizing for a man who never would. She is warm and friendly and assumes the best of everyone until they prove otherwise. Many first took her for a pariah, but good word has spread, and she is now well-liked.

A gifted duelist, the Flagellant would rather talk a foe into yielding than kill them. If all else fails, she fights with an estoc and a parrying dagger.

She has begun to suspect that there are bad actors among the villagers, or some force compelling them. She has found villagers crouched in the corners of their homes, wailing in terror. She has seen them beating their heads against the gates at night. One tried to bite her.

What frightens her most is not the villagers, but how familiar they sound. At night, when the village is quiet, she hears something too.

## Secret

She killed the so-called "great knight." One night, as he slept off another day of cruelty, she drove a blade into him and took his armor for her own. She has taken one life in its sleep, and she will not take another lightly.

But something deep in the soil of the Wytchwood would not let him go so easily. His body twisted into a thorned, plant-like thing and wound itself around her head, and it has never let go. What remains of him hates her mercy. Every time she spares a foe, the thorns tighten and draw blood. She has chosen to bear it. Each act of kindness costs her, and she pays gladly, because the alternative is becoming him.

The voice she hears at night is his. Since the Green Mother's power began to swell, the thorns have grown, and he speaks more clearly than he has in years. Whatever is whispering to the villagers has found its way into the thorns, and through them, into her.
