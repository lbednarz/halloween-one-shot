name: The Summoner
epithet: Warlock (Fiend, Pact of the Chain)
summary: A warlock who carries a demon in a flask, kept in check by rituals she never breaks. She finds more peace among the Wytchwood's monsters than in the village.
---

A wielder of magic in ornate clothing, the Summoner has made a pact. Around her neck hangs a small metal flask, marked with a circle beneath a half-moon that curves above it like a pair of horns. Within the flask lives a physically imposing, tricky demon. The Summoner consults with it, walks the woodland with it, and lets it calm the hearts of the monsters there.

The demon prefers the power of suggestion, or a gruesome, clawed strike when its persuasion lands on deaf ears. The Summoner rarely releases it within the village. Past carelessness led to bloodbaths among the guard, and to folklore about a pale woman carrying a fiend in a bottle, seeking blood to offer her god of war. She has wandered far from her homeland to escape those stories. Here in Wickermoor Hollow, the retellings have mangled the details so badly that no villager could possibly recognize her.

The Summoner is extremely particular. She lights her candles at first light and lets each burn exactly halfway, but always discards them before they reach a quarter of their length. She counts the steps from one end of the barracks to the other, and if it takes more than 500, she returns to her room. The list goes on. She keeps these rituals because the flask's magic is temperamental: each time she opens it, there is a chance the demon will not be compelled to return.

She is friendly, but far bolder in conversation when the demon is with her, whether in spirit or in body. She finds more peace in the woods than in the village, and even makes friends among the monstrosities there. She believes they are tortured beings, warped from their original, delicate forms.

## Secret

The demon in the flask is her brother. Or it is partly him.

When they were young, he fell to a wasting sickness no healer could cure. In desperation she made her pact, and the price of saving him was a body to keep his soul in: the long-dead husk of a demon, raised and refilled. He came back. So did whatever had once lived in it. Now the two share one mind, and neither is fully in control.

Her rituals are not superstition. Each candle, each counted step, is part of the binding that keeps her brother uppermost. The bloodbaths the stories speak of were the nights the demon won. Every time she opens the flask, she does not know which of them will answer.

This is why she cannot see the monsters of the Wytchwood as monsters. She looks at them and sees him: something gentle, buried under something terrible, waiting to be found.
