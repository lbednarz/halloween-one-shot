---
name: The Knight-errant
aka: Faylese Naal
epithet: Paladin (Oath of the Ancients)
---

A foolhardy warrior of the light, the Knight-errant came to Wickermoor Hollow to cleave the darkness from it one good deed at a time. That was the plan, anyway. For most of her time in Wickermoor Village, her "quests" have been couriering parcels from one end of town to the other and fixing creaking doors and wobbly tables for the elderly.

She is hard to miss: a slight elf in battered half-plate, hauling a greatsword nearly as tall as she is. And lately, when she runs, pale, ghostly wings unfurl from her back.

Everyone in the village knows the story. A mutated beast came prowling at the edge of town, and the Knight-errant went out alone to slay it. She came back covered in black blood, and within days she had wings. The villagers say the Green Mother rewarded her. They toast her in the tavern, children trail after her in the street, and the governor likes to have her standing beside him when he speaks.

She is earnest, kind, and terrible at accepting praise. She still runs errands nobody asks her to, and she changes the subject whenever the beast comes up. The Swordsaint has been watching her train with an appraising eye, and the Squire follows the same path of chores and small kindnesses she once did.

She fights with more heart than technique: wide, heavy swings of her greatsword, vines that rise from the earth at her call, and, when she needs it, a column of cold, pale light.

Plucky errand girl yesterday, village hero today. But her magic keeps drawing her back toward the Wytchwood, and she has started to listen.

## Secret

She didn't kill it. The beast carried its young clinging to its body, and in an earnest, clumsy battle, her blow found the child instead. The beast, stricken with grief, fled with its crippled young back into the Wytchwood. She can only hope it never returns. Every toast in her honor is a lie she has let stand.

The black blood was the young beast's. It seeped into her skin, and a searing pain followed; she was sure it would be her end. Instead she rose with a strange, fae-like magic coursing through her. The wings are not a blessing. They are what the blood made her.

The light she calls down in battle is pale and cold, like moonlight, and it is strongest on the nights everyone is told not to look up. Is the pull toward the Wytchwood some ploy of the grieving beast, or her own new nature calling her to meet her kin?
